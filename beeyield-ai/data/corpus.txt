Title: American Foulbrood (AFB)
Scientific Name: Paenibacillus larvae
Category: Bacterial
Risk Level: CRITICAL
Signs: Sunken, dark, perforated cappings; 'pupal tongue' protruding from remains.
Symptoms: Larvae turn coffee-brown and ropy; distinct 'foul' decayed-fish odor. Scale adheres tightly to cell wall.
Causes: Spore-forming bacteria ingested by young larvae. Spores remain viable for 40+ years.
Detection: Rope Test (>2cm stretch); Holst Milk Test; EFD Diagnostic Kits.
Treatment: Usage of antibiotics (Terramycin) only suppresses symptoms. Total incineration of hive, bees, and frames is the only reliable cure.
Prevention: Regular inspection; tool sterilization; hygienic queens; avoiding buying used equipment without history.
Transmission: Robbing; drifting; contaminated tools; swapping frames between hives.
Myths: Freezing equipment kills spores (False); Honey from AFB hives is toxic to humans (False, but spreads disease to bees).
Action Steps: ['Execute Rope Test', 'Seal hive immediately', 'Notify veterinary authority', 'Burn hive in pit']

Title: European Foulbrood (EFB)
Scientific Name: Melissococcus plutonius
Category: Bacterial
Risk Level: HIGH
Signs: Twisted, yellow/brown larvae coiled in 'C' shape; die before capping.
Symptoms: Melted/rubbery larvae; sour/yeasty smell; spotty brood pattern. Tracheal system often visible.
Causes: Bacterial infection exacerbated by nutritional stress or chilled brood.
Detection: Visual inspection; EFB diagnostic kits.
Treatment: Re-queening (break brood cycle); Shook Swarm method; antibiotic treatment (Oxytetracycline) where legal.
Prevention: Maintain strong colonies; ensure adequate pollen/nectar flow; reduce stress.
Transmission: Nurse bees feeding larvae; drifting; contaminated pollen.
Myths: It always kills the colony (False, strong colonies can clear it); It smells like fish (False, smells sour).

Risk Level: CRITICAL
